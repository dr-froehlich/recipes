## Context

REQ-006 put the household's collection into Tandoor — 270 recipes, 2639 ingredient rows,
0 errors. The structure came through intact. What came through *misparsed* is the inside of
the ingredient lines:

```
"1 reife Avocado"        -> amount 1, unit "reife",     food "Avocado"
"2 Scheiben altes Brot"  -> amount 2, unit "Scheiben",  food "altes Brot"
"1 gehäufter Tl Senf"    -> amount 1, unit "gehäufter", food "Tl Senf"
"1 Eigelb zum Bestreichen" -> amount 1, unit "Eigelb",  food "zum Bestreichen"
```

`IngredientParser` takes the token after the amount as the unit. When that token is a German
adjective the adjective becomes a unit and the *real* unit is pushed into the food name; when
it is a noun the food is pushed out entirely, so the last line above contains no egg yolk at
all. Measured over the 2759 real ingredient lines: **844 distinct foods and 105 distinct
units**, of which 30 units are adjectives, 7 are range fragments, and 513 foods are used
exactly once. `IngredientParser.get_food` matches **exactly** on `name` or `plural_name` —
there is no fuzzy or partial matching — so `Avocado` and `Avocados` are two foods, and
`Tl Senf` will never meet `Senf`. Nothing rolls up on a shopping list.

### Where the seam goes — reworked 2026-08-14

The first implementation of this REQ was built on the rule *the food is the last capitalised
noun; everything that qualifies it becomes a note*, and the owner **declined its validation**.
The reason is a fact about Tandoor that the frozen text never checked:

> **The shopping list renders amount, unit and food, and never the note.**
> `vue3/src/components/display/ShoppingLineItem.vue` does not mention the field.

So `Paprika` noted *rot* is a shopping list that says "Paprika". A qualifier that changes
*which item you pick up in the shop* has to be **in the food name** or it is gone at exactly
the moment it was needed. The rule inverts:

> **The food is what you buy, written out in full. What you *do* with it — and how you judge
> it at the shelf — becomes a note.**

Which side a qualifier lands on, all four sides ruled by the owner on 2026-08-14:

| line | food | note | why |
|---|---|---|---|
| `1 rote Paprika` | `Paprika rot` | — | you buy a red one |
| `1050er Weizenmehl` | `Weizenmehl 1050` | — | you buy that type |
| `500 g mageres Hackfleisch` | `Hackfleisch mager` | — | unlisted qualifier, **default is to keep it** |
| `100 g getrocknete Tomaten` | `Tomate getrocknet` | — | sold that way (`PRODUCT_FORMS`) |
| `1 Zwiebel, gehackt` | `Zwiebel` | *gehackt* | a participle: you do it at home |
| `1 reife Avocado` | `Avocado` | *reif* | a condition, judged at the shelf |
| `1 gehäufter Tl Senf` | `Senf` (1 Tl) | *gehäuft* | qualifies the spoon, not the mustard |
| `Zitrone, Saft von 1/2` | `Zitrone` (0,5) | *Saft von* | you buy the whole lemon |
| `Butter für die Form` | `Butter` | *für die Form* | purpose, not product |

Names come out **noun first and de-inflected** — `Weizenmehl 1050`, `Paprika rot` — so every
flour and every pepper sorts together and hangs under one parent in Tandoor's food tree. The
collection already writes the flour both ways, `1050er Weizenmehl` and `Weizenmehl (550er)`,
and both now arrive at the same food, so the two forms the household already uses agree with
each other rather than one being imposed on the other.

The **default direction is what changed**, and it is the point: a qualifier no list names stays
with the food. Nothing is silently dropped from the shopping list ever again. The price is a
longer food list — the first implementation's headline of 844 foods down to 577 is no longer a
goal, and no criterion here grades shrinkage — and that price is paid deliberately.

Measured over the whole collection after the rework — 342 documents, 2680 raw ingredient
lines — the repair takes **779 foods to 593** and leaves **33 units, every one of them named
by the repair file**. Two food names out of 2680 lines are still malformed, and both come from
source lines that are malformed themselves (a bulleted line that is only the character `1`, and
a truncated line with an unclosed bracket). The tree gathers 87 head nouns, among them
`Weizenmehl` over its three grades, `Paprika` over five colours and `Kartoffel` over its
cooking types.

A prototype over the real lines also found where the rule is not enough, and those failures
shape the requirement: a repair that fires on a line it should not can make that line *worse*
than the parser left it (`5–6 säuerliche Äpfel (z.B. Braeburn)` came out with the food
`Braeburn)`), which is a worse outcome than leaving it alone.

## Requirement

Ingredient lines in the household's Word documents resolve to **the food you would buy, named
in full**, with what you do with it in the note; the qualified foods hang under their head noun
in the food tree; and the deployed instance holds the result.

### The repair file

A committed file beside `cookbook/integration/word.py` holds the rule's data — closed lists,
not per-word repairs:

- **cut prepositions** — where a note of purpose or handling begins (`für`, `zum`, `mit`,
  `in`, `aus`, `vom`, …). `von` is **not** among them; it belongs to the extraction phrases.
  `typ` is **not** among them; it introduces a grade, which belongs in the food name.
- **cut conjunctions** — `oder`, `und`, which open a note only once the food has been named.
- **drop words** — filler and articles that say nothing about what to buy (`Etwas`, `Je`,
  `Verschiedene`, `Einige`, `Eventuell`, `ca.`, and the articles).
- **extraction nouns** — the part taken out of a whole product (`Saft`, `Schale`, `Abrieb`,
  `Mark`, …); the product stays the food and the part becomes the note.
- **condition words** — condition and measure, which are notes: `reif`, `alt`, `weich`,
  `frisch`, `groß`, `klein`, `gehäuft`, `gestrichen`, …
- **product forms** — the past participles that name something *sold* that way and therefore
  stay in the food name: `getrocknet`, `tiefgekühlt`, `gerieben`, `geräuchert`, `gemahlen`, …
- **known units** — what counts as a unit, including spelled-out forms (`Teelöffel`,
  `Esslöffel`) and their aliases (`EL` → `El`, `TL` → `Tl`, `Dosen` → `Dose`).
- **unit conversions** — `Tasse` → 180 ml, applied to the amount.
- **keep-whole** — the multi-word names whose first word is not the head noun.
- **food aliases** — the plural and spelling pairs (`Avocados` → `Avocado`).

There is **no adjective list**: a past participle is recognised by its shape, and every other
qualifier is kept with the food by default (Decision 13), so the collection's 141-word
adjective tail needs no entries at all.

### The repair

Before parsing, the importer normalises en-dash ranges so the parser's own range handling
fires, and splits a `Je X und Y <Noun>` line into one line per variant.

After parsing, it repairs the result:

- a **unit that is not a known unit** is not a unit; the word is handed back to the front of
  the food phrase rather than classified in the unit slot, because only the food repair knows
  whether a qualifier belongs in the name or the note;
- a **known unit stranded in the food** becomes the unit, even with a qualifier in front of it;
- an **amount, grade or lone qualifier the parser parked in a note** is taken back out of it;
- in the food phrase, everything from the first cut word onward becomes a note, filler and
  articles are dropped, the head noun is the last capitalised token that is not an extraction
  noun, and each qualifier in front of it is sorted by Decision 14 into the note or into the
  name; a **post-nominal** token is kept exactly as written;
- the food is written **head noun first**, aliased, with its qualifiers behind it, de-inflected;
- a phrase offering a **choice of qualifiers** (`frische oder getrocknete Petersilie`) keeps
  the bare noun as the food and puts the whole choice in the note, because picking one would
  put a guess on the shopping list;
- unit conversions are applied last.

A line that contains nothing the lists name comes out **byte-identical**. No repair may lose a
qualifier that the source line carried: it is in the food name or it is in the note.

### The food tree

Each qualified food is parented under its head noun — the first token of its repaired name —
and the parent gets `substitute_children`. A parent is created even when no line uses the bare
noun. A name whose second token is capitalised is a name rather than a noun plus qualifiers,
and stays at the root. Nothing is written to the `Ingredient` rows for any of this, and the
import does not warn about it.

### The collection

The develop session runs the importer over the whole collection, builds the lists from what
that turns up, and iterates until the yield is right. It then restores the pre-import dump on
the deployed host and re-imports, so the live instance holds the repaired result.

## Notes

**Sequencing — REQ-006's validation must run first.** REQ-006's AC5 is graded against the
import that is live now (`Import 5`, 270 recipes, three named recipes recorded in
`.devsteward/evidence/REQ-006/import-source.json`). Decision 9 restores the database and
re-imports, which changes the keyword and the counts and would leave that evidence describing
a state that no longer exists. Run `steward validate REQ-006` **before** starting this REQ's
develop step. The `depends_on: REQ-006` above enforces the ordering mechanically.

**Rulings taken during develop (2026-08-14, first attempt).** Three, all put to the owner
because the frozen text contradicted itself or the rulings file. (1) *Grades in notes*: AC3
asked for a bare number and AC5 for a byte-identical result on the same line shape. (2) *`von`*:
listed as a cut word, but cutting there keeps `Saft` and `Schale` as foods against the ruling
`Saft einer Limette → Limette · Saft`. Ruled — `von` is not a cut word; `vom` still is.
(3) *`Weizenmehl Typ 550`*: keep-whole and `typ`-as-cut-word disagree. Ruled — cut at `Typ`.
Rulings 2 and 3 stand. Ruling 1 is **superseded** by the rework: the grade is not in a note at
all any more, it is in the food name (Decision 6).

**The rework (2026-08-14, second attempt).** AC7's sign-off was **declined** by the owner, on
the grounds that the specification was wrong rather than the implementation. The shopping list
does not render notes, so the first rule's seam threw away exactly the information the shopping
list exists to carry. Eight further questions were put and ruled, and they are Decisions 13–16
plus the AC rewrite above. The whole acceptance block was rewritten with them: AC1 now grades
the *keep* default, AC5 grades "no qualifier is lost" instead of byte-identity alone, AC7 is new
and grades the food tree, and the old AC7 sign-off is now AC8 with the food-count ceiling
removed — a flour is now correctly several foods, and the old criterion would have graded the
fix as a failure.

**Known residual failures.** The intake prototype left four. Three are fixed by the rework:
`5–6 säuerliche Äpfel (z.B. Braeburn)` now yields `Apfel säuerlich` noted `5 - 6` (AC5);
`1 gehäufter Teelöffel Salz` recovers its unit from the spelled-out form (AC2); and
`Mark von 1 Vanilleschote` resolves to `Vanilleschote` noted `Mark von`, because the phrase is
put back into written order before the head noun is chosen (AC2). One remains, and is accepted
rather than fixed: `1050er Weizen- oder Dinkelmehl` names two flours in one line, and resolves
to `Dinkelmehl 1050` noted `oder Weizen-`. The elided compound cannot be expanded without
knowing the head noun's stem, and choosing the first alternative would put a guess on the
shopping list.

The parser's habit of reading *through* a bracket — dropping `(z.B. Braeburn)` entirely, and
taking its amount and unit out of `(es geht auch 1 El getrockneter Thymian)` — was recorded at
intake as not this rule's to undo. The corpus run overturned that: it is undone, by reading the
bracket off the line rather than out of the parse (Decision 17).

**The seven bulleted instruction paragraphs.** REQ-006 found seven lines that are instructions
accidentally bulleted in Word. They are noise before the repair and noise after it; no
criterion here is graded on them.

**Required environment.** AC1-AC7 need only what a clean checkout has: pytest-django against
SQLite, plus the `python-docx` dependency REQ-006 added. None can pass by skipping. The
fixtures are written for the tests and mirror the collection's conventions; no document from
the real collection is committed and no real ingredient line is asserted against in the gate.
AC8's evidence records real ingredient lines, which the owner has agreed to.

**Chefkoch documents.** Declined permanently (Decision 12). REQ-006's plan recorded them as a
gap to be closed by re-saving them in Word; that is superseded here.
