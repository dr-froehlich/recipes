## Context

REQ-006 put the household's collection into Tandoor — 270 recipes, 2639 ingredient rows,
0 errors. The structure came through intact. What came through *misparsed* is the inside of
the ingredient lines:

```
"1 reife Avocado"        -> amount 1, unit "reife",     food "Avocado"
"2 Scheiben altes Brot"  -> amount 2, unit "Scheiben",  food "altes Brot"
"1 gehäufter Tl Senf"    -> amount 1, unit "gehäufter", food "Tl Senf"
"1 Eigelb zum Bestreichen" -> amount 1, unit "Eigelb",  food "zum Bestreichen"
```

`IngredientParser` takes the token after the amount as the unit. When that token is a German
adjective the adjective becomes a unit and the *real* unit is pushed into the food name; when
it is a noun the food is pushed out entirely, so the last line above contains no egg yolk at
all. Measured over the 2759 real ingredient lines: **844 distinct foods and 105 distinct
units**, of which 30 units are adjectives, 7 are range fragments, and 513 foods are used
exactly once. `IngredientParser.get_food` matches **exactly** on `name` or `plural_name` —
there is no fuzzy or partial matching — so `Avocado` and `Avocados` are two foods, and
`Tl Senf` will never meet `Senf`. Nothing rolls up on a shopping list.

The owner ruled on a sample of cases at intake, and the rulings turned out to share a single
rule rather than needing a repair per word:

| ruled | becomes |
|---|---|
| `Saft einer Limette` | `Limette` · *Saft* |
| `Abgeriebene Schale einer Zitrone` | `Zitrone` · *Schalenabrieb* |
| `4%ige Natronlauge` | `Natronlauge` · *4%* |
| `1050er Weizenmehl` | `Weizenmehl` · *1050* |
| `flüssiger Honig` | `Honig` · *flüssig* |
| `Verschiedene Kräuter` | `Kräuter` |

The rule those share: **the food is the thing you buy — the last capitalised noun; everything
that qualifies it becomes a note.** German does the work, because attributive adjectives are
lowercase and the noun is capitalised. That single rule plus four closed lists replaces the
~250-entry per-word list this REQ was first drafted around.

Measured over all 2759 lines, the repair takes **844 foods to 577** and **105 units to 41**,
and the 41 that survive are all genuine kitchen units. Nine separate flour foods
(`Weizenmehl`, `1050er Weizenmehl`, `550er Weizenmehl`, `405er Weizenmehl`,
`Weizenmehl Typ 550`, and the Roggen/Dinkel equivalents) become three. The collection already
writes it this way where it uses parentheses — `200 g Weizenmehl (550er)` parses today to
food `Weizenmehl`, note `550er` — so this makes the two forms the household already uses
agree with each other rather than imposing a convention on either.

A prototype over the real lines also found where the rule is not enough, and those failures
shape the requirement: a repair that fires on a line it should not can make that line *worse*
than the parser left it (`5–6 säuerliche Äpfel (z.B. Braeburn)` came out with the food
`Braeburn)`), which is a worse outcome than leaving it alone.

## Decisions

| # | Decision | Rationale |
|---|----------|-----------|
| 1 | The repair lives in **the Word importer**, not in the shared `IngredientParser` | The alternative — a new automation type on the raw line — touches `models.py`, a migration, the serializer, the frontend automation editor and the parser. Real core divergence against REQ-001 Decision 5, for a problem that is entirely this collection's |
| 2 | **One rule plus four closed lists**, not a per-word repair list | Intake began with an enumerated list of every observed string; the owner's rulings collapsed it. Cut at a preposition, drop filler, food starts at the last capitalised token. ~40 lines of data instead of ~250, and the 141-word tail of adjectives falls out with no entries at all |
| 3 | The repair runs **after parsing**, not as a rewrite of the raw line | Post-parse we know what the parser thought each token was, so "this unit is not a unit" and "a real unit is stranded at the head of the food" are both decidable. Pre-parse they are guesses. Two line-level fixes are the exception (Decision 4) because they change tokenisation itself |
| 4 | Exactly two fixes run **before** parsing: en-dash ranges and the `Je X und Y` split | `4–6 Eier` must become `4 - 6 Eier` for the parser's own range handling to fire, and `Je 1 rote und gelbe Paprika` must become two lines. Neither is expressible after tokenising |
| 5 | The lists live in **one committed file** beside the importer, not Automation rows | Decision 9 restores the pre-import dump before re-importing, and a restore rolls the whole database back — Automation rows created after the dump would be silently wiped, so every re-import would need them re-created first or would quietly produce the old result |
| 6 | A **grade keeps its number and loses its German suffix**: `1050er` → `1050`, `4%ige` → `4%` | Ruled by the owner. The grade is data, not an adjective, and `Weizenmehl · 1050` matches how the collection already writes `Weizenmehl (550er)` |
| 7 | A short **keep-whole list** overrides the rule for genuine multi-word names | `Wiener Würstchen` (ruled), `Paprikapulver edelsüß` and `Weizenmehl Typ 550` put the qualifier where the rule cannot see it. `Crème fraîche` needs no entry — the rule already leaves it alone |
| 8 | A repair that would make a line **worse is not applied** | The prototype turned `5–6 säuerliche Äpfel (z.B. Braeburn)` into the food `Braeburn)`. A line the parser already handled correctly must come out unchanged; leaving a line alone is always available and always safe |
| 9 | The existing data is fixed by **restoring the pre-import dump and re-importing** | The dump from REQ-006's deploy is on disk and verified restorable. A clean re-run repairs every row and is reproducible; merging afterwards cannot recover a note that was never parsed. Cost: instance edits made after the import are lost, which is accepted |
| 10 | The repair list is **produced by running the corpus**, and that run is a develop-session obligation | Its entries are exactly what 342 real documents produced. It cannot be written at a desk, and the System Tester can neither produce it nor fix what producing it reveals |
| 11 | Repairs apply **only to the Word importer**, not to URL import or manual entry | Those paths are not where the collection comes from, and making them global means the shared parser again (Decision 1). Recorded so the asymmetry is deliberate |
| 12 | The **ten table-shaped Chefkoch documents are never imported** | Reaffirmed twice by the owner. This closes REQ-006's known gap as *declined*, not deferred: no follow-on REQ, no second parser profile, and the operator's zip excludes them permanently |

## Requirement

Ingredient lines in the household's Word documents resolve to the food you would buy, with
everything that qualifies it in the note, and the deployed instance holds the result.

### The repair file

A committed file beside `cookbook/integration/word.py` holds the rule's data — closed lists,
not per-word repairs:

- **cut words** — the prepositions and conjunctions at which a note begins (`für`, `zum`,
  `von`, `mit`, `in`, `aus`, `oder`, `und`, `typ`, …).
- **drop words** — filler and articles that say nothing about what to buy (`Etwas`, `Je`,
  `Verschiedene`, `Einige`, `Eventuell`, `ca.`, and the articles).
- **known units** — what counts as a unit, including spelled-out forms (`Teelöffel`,
  `Esslöffel`) and their aliases (`EL` → `El`, `TL` → `Tl`, `Dosen` → `Dose`).
- **unit conversions** — `Tasse` → 180 ml, applied to the amount.
- **keep-whole** — the handful of multi-word names the rule must not split.
- **food aliases** — the plural and spelling pairs (`Avocados` → `Avocado`).

### The repair

Before parsing, the importer normalises en-dash ranges so the parser's own range handling
fires, and splits a `Je X und Y <Noun>` line into one line per variant.

After parsing, it repairs the result:

- a **unit that is not a known unit** is not a unit: a lowercase one is an adjective and
  becomes a note; a capitalised one is a noun and rejoins the food;
- a **known unit stranded at the head of the food** becomes the unit;
- in the food phrase, everything from the first cut word onward becomes a note, filler and
  articles are dropped, and the food is what remains **from the last capitalised token
  onward** — earlier tokens become notes in the order they appeared;
- notes keep capitalised words as written, de-inflect lowercase adjectives, and reduce a
  grade to its number;
- unit conversions and aliases are applied last.

A line that contains nothing the lists name comes out **byte-identical**, and a line whose
food the parser already resolved correctly is never made worse.

### The collection

The develop session runs the importer over the whole collection, builds the lists from what
that turns up, and iterates until the yield is right. It then restores the pre-import dump on
the deployed host and re-imports, so the live instance holds the repaired result.

## Notes

**Sequencing — REQ-006's validation must run first.** REQ-006's AC5 is graded against the
import that is live now (`Import 5`, 270 recipes, three named recipes recorded in
`.devsteward/evidence/REQ-006/import-source.json`). Decision 9 restores the database and
re-imports, which changes the keyword and the counts and would leave that evidence describing
a state that no longer exists. Run `steward validate REQ-006` **before** starting this REQ's
develop step. The `depends_on: REQ-006` above enforces the ordering mechanically.

**Rulings taken during develop (2026-08-14).** Three, all put to the owner because the frozen
text contradicted itself or the rulings file. (1) *Grades in notes*: AC3 asks for a bare number
and AC5 asks for a byte-identical result on the same line shape. Ruled — a grade reads as a
bare number wherever it ends up, including in a note the parser itself wrote, so AC5's
unchanged set is graded on a plain `375 g Weizenmehl` and the grade rewrite is asserted
explicitly instead. (2) *`von`*: listed as a cut word, but cutting there keeps `Saft` and
`Schale` as foods against the ruling `Saft einer Limette → Limette · Saft`. Ruled — `von` is
not a cut word; `vom` still is. (3) *`Weizenmehl Typ 550`*: keep-whole and `typ`-as-cut-word
disagree. Ruled — cut, and the note reads `550`, not `Typ 550`. All three are carried out in
[the plan](plans/0007-import-repairs.md).

**Known residual failures.** The intake prototype left four, all reproduced from real lines
and all owed a fix or an explicit exception during develop, not a silent pass:
`5–6 säuerliche Äpfel (z.B. Braeburn)` becomes the food `Braeburn)` when the range and the
parenthetical interact (AC5 exists to fail exactly this); `1 gehäufter Teelöffel Salz` loses
its unit because `Teelöffel` was not in the known-units list; `Mark von 1 Vanilleschote`
resolves to `Mark` rather than `Vanilleschote`, because the parser's own transposition has
already reordered the line before the repair sees it; and `1050er Weizen- oder Dinkelmehl`
names two flours in one line and cannot resolve to a single food.

**The seven bulleted instruction paragraphs.** REQ-006 found seven lines that are instructions
accidentally bulleted in Word. They are noise before the repair and noise after it; no
criterion here is graded on them.

**Required environment.** AC1-AC6 need only what a clean checkout has: pytest-django against
SQLite, plus the `python-docx` dependency REQ-006 added. None can pass by skipping. The
fixtures are written for the tests and mirror the collection's conventions; no document from
the real collection is committed and no real ingredient line is asserted against in the gate.
AC7's evidence records real ingredient lines, which the owner has agreed to.

**Chefkoch documents.** Declined permanently (Decision 12). REQ-006's plan recorded them as a
gap to be closed by re-saving them in Word; that is superseded here.

**Upstream.** Not offered. The rule encodes German capitalisation and one household's
vocabulary, and it lives in an importer that is itself fork-local. The rejected alternative —
a new automation type on the shared parser — *would* have been upstream-shaped, and is
recorded in Decision 1 in case that trade ever looks different.
